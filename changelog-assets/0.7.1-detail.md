## Where a convergent step is drawn

The activity walk had no notion of where a decision's branches meet. Its loop guard was "keep going
until a step has already been drawn", so a branch walked straight through the meeting point and took
the rest of the graph with it. Where the outermost decision enclosed everything, the whole process was
drawn inside one arm and the other arm dead-ended.

`_step_graph` answers where a set of branches converges, and the answer is **post-dominance** rather
than reachability. The distinction carries the fix: a step both arms *can* reach may still be escaped
by a path through one of them, and hoisting such a step past the `endif` would draw it on a path that
never had it. A convergence point is one every branch reaches and that no path out of the branching
step gets past, which is what makes drawing it once, after the construct, faithful. The same rule
serves a fork, whose branches are drawn past `end fork` whether or not the model declares a join step
for them to meet at.

Four shapes, and where each is drawn:

| Shape | Where it is drawn |
| --- | --- |
| both arms of one decision reach it | after that decision's `endif` |
| a fork's branches meet at a declared join | after `end fork` |
| a fork's branches meet at a plain action | after `end fork` — a join entry is not required |
| the arrivals are at different nesting depths | in each branch that arrives at it, along with whatever follows it there |

The fourth is the residue: no placement in an `if`/`else` tree covers exactly those paths and no
others, so the step is drawn in each branch that arrives at it. Both drawings carry the same
`arch://` sentinel, so both are selectable and both resolve to the one artifact — which is what makes
two drawings of one step readable rather than confusing.

Two alternatives were tried and neither survives contact with the viewer. `label` / `goto` would
state the arrival directly, and on the pinned PlantUML 1.2026.3 both are accepted and then ignored: a
backward `goto` draws an arrow to the following node, and inside a branch it leaves a dangling
arrowhead. A flowchart connector pair renders correctly and is unreadable here, because it puts an
unlabelled circle into a surface where every element is expected to be clickable and to resolve to
the artifact it stands for, and the circle resolves to nothing.

What bounds the duplication is that the walk carries the chain of steps it is currently inside: a
step already on that chain ends the walk rather than being drawn again. So a `step-flow` that closes
a loop is **not drawn** — every step of the loop is, which a cyclic diagram did not manage before,
but the edge back has no rendering here yet.

Two further defects in the same walk drew nothing at all rather than drawing the wrong thing. A back
edge leaves no step that nothing enters, so the walk had no root and emitted `start` / `stop` with
nothing between. And the pass over steps the root walk did not reach ran only when there was no root,
so a second declared chain went undrawn; it now runs whenever any step remains.

### What the gate asserts

Every declared step is drawn. No step is drawn more often than what leads to it is drawn, which is
the ceiling that separates a second arrival from the fork multiplication where one tail appeared once
per branch. Every drawing is the step itself, never a connector or a jump. Every branch reaches its
own first step. What follows a join sits where its fork closes. And a step every arm of a decision
reaches is drawn in all of those arms or in none of them, never in some — that last one is a picture
saying one path carries on and the other stops, which is the defect this release exists for.

Coverage is scoped to the step kinds that emit a labelled line, because PlantUML's `fork` keyword
takes no label or link argument, so a fork carries nothing to read back.

Two of the shapes it is stated over exist because a diagram whose forks are all at top level cannot
distinguish a repaired walk from a broken one: appending the leftover steps to the end of the body
lands in exactly the right place. A nested fork and a loop declared from its middle separate them.

`tests/diagram_types/activity_golden_bodies.txt` records the rendered body of every shape, so a change
to the walk is a diff rather than a discovery. The walk moved to its own module in a commit of its
own, with that file as the guard on the move.

## Reading a step's identity back off a line

W045 asks whether a **stored** body draws a declared step. Re-rendering from `diagram-entities` and
comparing would put the renderer against itself and pass on every input, which is the staleness the
diagnostic exists to catch, so it reads the body as stored. Two contracts were completed to allow
that.

`BaseDiagramVerificationContext` now carries the body, filled by the runner from the content the
verifier has already read. The alternative — a contribution re-reading the file from `ctx.loc` — would
spell the frontmatter fence a fifteenth time.

Reading a step's identity back off a rendered line has an owner: `sentinel_of` and `drawn_step_ids`
sit beside the two functions that write it, and `tests/architecture/test_each_syntax_has_one_reader.py`
carries the row. The pair is asserted together over what the syntax permits — both emission forms, an
id carrying the one escaped character, a user link alongside, a step bound to an entity — because a
writer checked against one fixture and a reader against another can agree with nothing.

The diagnostic sees an omitted step, not a misplaced one. Presence is readable off a line; placement
needs the body's nesting, and that reading lives only in the renderer's own gate.

## Reconciling what a diagram says it draws

Both ways a body arrives at an edit — rendered from `diagram-entities`, or supplied as `puml` — ended
by unioning the stored reference set with what the new body draws. Union is right for combining a
caller's own argument with what a fresh artifact draws, and wrong for carrying a stored value across a
replacement. The two were conflated one line above the merge that then could not tell them apart, so
the caller's argument and the file's own value have separate names and only the second is reconciled.

The reconcile is stated once, after both branches, because both hand it the same question. What they
bring differs: a rendered body brings what the renderer drew and nothing it was unsure about; a
supplied body brings what was read off it plus the pairs the reader could not name.

Three things let it be decisive:

- **Inference says what it could not decide.** It was already deliberately silent where a drawn glyph
  fits more than one connection. It now reports those pairs, so silence about a pair is not read as
  evidence against it.
- **Nesting is read transitively.** `_containment_relations` reads one level, which is the level the
  completeness rules compare and is unchanged. A body nesting an element two levels down also states
  that the outermost contains it, and `indirect_nesting_relations` states the ancestry above the
  immediate parent, with an unaliased grouping transparent as it is there.
- **Endpoints are compared through `parse_connection_id`.** The `source---target@@type` form is not
  spelled a second time, because a plain `find("---")` matches the hyphen inside a slug.

A reference is dropped only where all three hold: both endpoints are among the entities the body
declares, the pair is not one the reader could not decide, and the reference is not among what the
body draws.

Connections only. The entity side refuses the equivalent write already — **E309** fires when
`entity-ids-used` names an entity whose alias the body does not declare — so there is no silent drift
there, and reconciling it would turn a refusal into an automatic rewrite of the author's frontmatter.
The asymmetry is that the connection side had no such rule; E316 catches only the inverse, a relation
drawn but unlisted.

`puml="auto-sync"` does not do this and is not meant to. It restores consistency by redrawing the
missing edge, which resolves the disagreement in favour of the stored reference rather than the body.
On a `manual-layout` diagram the union is kept deliberately, because there the picture is the
author's.

## What may host an artifact

The deployment projection listed five host types and the relationship table permitted the first hop of
the deployment chain from one of them. One row states it for the class the five share, in the
vocabulary the table already speaks, so one row replaces four:

```yaml
- ["@technology-internal-active-structure-element", artifact, [assignment]]
- [technology-node, artifact, [aggregation]]
```

Aggregation stays on the pair that already had it and is not widened alongside assignment. The models
it exists for can only have used that pair, since it was the only one ever permitted, and widening it
would break the composition-mirrors-aggregation invariant (ArchiMate 4 §5.1.2) in four new places —
whose one documented exception is that pair.

`_DEPLOYMENT_HOST_TYPES` is gone. The host set derives from the class that defines it, through the
port that already answers class membership, and a derived set cannot drift from the table.

`_HOSTING_TYPES` becomes two sets, because it answered two questions with one literal.
`_ARTIFACT_HOSTING_TYPES` is what puts an artifact on a host: assignment and aggregation, with
composition dropped because the table permits it from no host into an artifact, so no valid model can
hold one. `_NODE_CONTAINMENT_TYPES` is what puts one host inside another: composition and aggregation,
and composition belongs there. A single shrunken set flattens every deployment view into one box,
which says the containers run side by side on a machine rather than together inside a runtime.

The gates are three, one per pair, deliberately not combined. A single gate over "the hosting types"
would make one pair's requirement drive the other pair's fix, and would drive the wrong one.

Reading `serving` as hosting is a standing non-goal with a test that says so. It is the only direct
technology-to-application rule, so it looks like the obvious substitute, and it means the opposite:
ArchiMate separates assignment, which allocates, from serving, which consumes.

## One specification per format facet

A shipped description said `format: uri` was informative only and that any string was accepted, while
the checker refused values. The description and the checker were two statements of one rule, in two
layers, free to drift — and they had drifted once already, the correction having been written into a
materialised copy without reaching the default it was copied from.

`format_rules` specifies each format as the procedure that decides it: the alternatives a value may
satisfy, one predicate each, any of them sufficing. The application's checker runs that rule instead
of holding its own copy, and both the shipped description and the refusal message are composed from
the rule's own terms, so neither can say something the procedure does not do.

There is no equality gate between a materialised copy and the shipped default.
`DefaultSchemataEnsureStep` documents a divergent copy as a supported state, since an operator's local
edit is never overwritten, so such a gate would turn a future customisation into a build failure. What
the gate asserts instead holds regardless: every declared format is one the checker runs, a
description saying what its format accepts says it in the rule's terms, and no shipped description
claims a format is unenforced. It is stated over the whole shipped schema set rather than the
ArchiMate subset of it.

## A gate that failed at random

`uv run zuban check` reported `Invalid type comment or annotation` on a file no change had touched, on
some runs and not others. Two modules named each other: the endpoint planner imports the refusal
wording at runtime, and the wording named the planner's own types under `TYPE_CHECKING`. That is

picked depended on what else it had already read. The value types both modules speak moved to

`tests/architecture/test_no_type_checking_import_cycles.py` refuses the shape, as the conservative

a latent hazard rather than a proven failure, and the one pre-existing pair carries an allowance with
what is known about it.

## Recording what a replace destroyed

The archive records twenty-odd operations — a created node, an edited field, an assigned provenance, a
sealed baseline, a shredded record. It did not record `import`, which is the one operation that
removes all of them at once.

The consequence is not a missing line. A re-seed left the chain reading *analysis created, nodes
assigned provenance*, and then nothing to explain why the store showed neither: effects whose cause is
absent, which is the reading a tamper-evident log exists to make impossible. It is also the mechanism
that discharges the Art. 12 obligation, whose constraint is stated without exception.

The entry is appended through the chained writer in the same transaction as the rows it describes,
rather than committed beside them, so a recorded import cannot outlive a failed one. Its payload
carries the bundle's file name — the name, not the path, because the entry is read off the machine
that wrote it — whether the graph was replaced, the per-table counts the replace destroyed, and the
per-section counts written.

`audit_log` is absent from both the import's section list and its delete order, and the comment there
now says that both absences are deliberate: the chain belongs to the store rather than to the graph it
describes. So an import neither clears the history that is there — which is what makes a re-seed
auditable instead of a gap — nor carries another store's hashes in and presents them as this one's.
Both halves are asserted, the second against a bundle offering an `audit_log` section.

Measured on the shipped store: the re-seed appends one entry naming 245 rows cleared across seven
tables and 245 written, and the chain verifies.

## Filling an anchor that was never set

`architecture_anchor_id` was documented optional at creation and immutable afterwards. Together those
left no route: an analysis created without one could never acquire one, and recreating it was barred
too, because provenance is immutable and its nodes cannot be re-filed under a replacement.

The distinction the immutability was making is between *moving* an anchor and *filling* one. Moving it
rewrites what an analysis was scoped to, and every finding under it was reached against the old
subject. Filling one that was never set rewrites nothing, because nothing was there. Only that
transition opens; moving and clearing are both refused, and restating the anchor already held is
accepted, since it asks for the state the analysis is in.

`ANALYSIS_FILLABLE_ONCE` and `permitted_analysis_updates` state this in the domain beside
`ANALYSIS_UPDATABLE`, and the two appliers ask it rather than each testing membership: the file stores
rewrite a record, the SQLCipher store builds an `UPDATE`, and the rule written twice would let one
backend admit what the other refused. The SQLCipher path reads its row first, because a fill-once
verdict depends on what is already there.

The refusal is a refusal rather than a silent drop. Every other non-updatable field is ignored, which
is right for one nothing can ever change; this one *can* be set, so ignoring the request would report
success on a write that did not happen. A refused anchor refuses the whole call — one request, one
verdict, so a caller can read the outcome from the refusal instead of re-reading the record.
`anchor_immutable` maps to `409 conflict`: the aggregate is in a state where the field no longer
moves, and nothing the caller sent is malformed.

## The other half of E316

E309 refuses an `entity-ids-used` entry whose alias the body does not declare. The connection side had
no equivalent — E315, E316 and E317 all read the body and check the frontmatter owns what it draws,
and nothing read the frontmatter and checked the body draws what it claims. So one disagreement was
policed in one direction, and the direction left open is a query surface: which views show this
connection.

Where the wrong claim persists is what makes it worth a diagnostic. A regenerating refresh redraws the
missing edge, so the claim becomes true again. A `manual-layout` diagram keeps its body verbatim and
unions the references, so it never does, and a hand-edited file never does either.

W307 is a warning. The rule is stated over live model content, and a repository that verified clean
must not fail over frontmatter nobody has touched — the same call W045 makes for the same reason.

Three things keep it from firing on legitimate content:

- **The judgement is the write path's own.** `body_contradicts_reference` moved to the domain, so the
  reconcile and the verifier cannot disagree about the same picture. An entry is contradicted only
  where the body declares both endpoints — it had the vocabulary to draw the relation — and draws
  nothing between them.
- **Indirect nesting counts as drawn.** A body nesting C two levels inside A draws
  `A --composition--> C` without stating that pair at any single level, so reading one level would
  report a correct entry as wrong.
- **An untyped arrow contributes every candidate on its pair.** It names none of them, so treating any
  as undrawn would read the reader's own silence as evidence. A typed arrow filters to its type, and
  that is what lets the rule conclude anything at all.

Measured over the repository: 50 diagrams, 33 in the rule's scope, 0 findings. Then one drawn relation
deleted from each of those 33 — 32 reported, and the 33rd correctly silent, because a reverse arrow
still draws that pair.

The rule's scope is the one E316 already has, and for the same reason: a diagram type that owns its
entities speaks its own body vocabulary, which the shared relation parser cannot read, so anything
concluded there would be an artefact of not understanding the body.

## Where a container runs, when it runs in more than one place

The deployment projection built its containment with `sorted(host_set)[:1]` — one home per container,
chosen by id order. On the shipped model the Artifact File Store is hosted by both the Developer
Workstation and the Repository Data Volume, so the volume was drawn holding nothing.

Truncating is not obviously wrong, and that is what let it survive. `_nest` resolves containment as a
tree because PUML declares an alias in exactly one place, so one child genuinely has one parent. What
the truncation threw away was not the second placement — that was never drawable — but the *choice*,
made before the view knew which hosts it was keeping. Narrow a view to one topology and the chosen
host is the one that got filtered out: the container then has no surviving parent and floats to the top
level, beside the host that does hold it.

So the projection reports every placement and the resolver picks among the ones it is drawing. Where
several survive, the first by id wins — deterministic and independent of the order the pairs arrive
in, so a redraw does not move a box, and an unfiltered view is unchanged. The nesting moved to
`_resolve_nesting`, which put `_resolve_model` back inside the file-length policy.

A second symptom shared the same box and was a different defect: the C4 macro table had no row for
`node`, so a host with no children fell through to the fallback whose own comment reads *unknown item
type → generic container shape*. A host with children never reaches that table — `_open_boundary`
emits the node macro for it — which is why this only ever showed on an empty one. Both the store
variant and the external suffix are refused for a node: a node is not a database whatever it holds, and
`Deployment_Node_Ext` is not defined by the C4 deployment stdlib. Verified by rendering on the pinned
PlantUML before the row was written — a body calling `_Ext` reports errors and emits no diagram, while
a childless `Deployment_Node` renders as a «node» box.

## Two deployment topologies, two views

The platform is deployed two ways and the model held one of them. Nothing on the Developer Workstation
realized the backend or the authoring tool: the container image was their only realizer and it is
aggregated by the App Container, so a deployment view of a workstation drew the two stores and neither
of the things a developer runs. `Architectonic Source Distribution` is the second artifact realizing
both — which of them a host holds is what makes it one topology or the other. The artifact index also
gained a home, on the backend state volume rather than beside the repository it indexes.

They are two views rather than one because the topologies are alternatives, not layers: a picture
holding both says the containers run in all those places at once. Each names the other's hosts under
`_excluded_entity_ids`, which the C4 levels already had — no new scoping key. The cost is that adding
a host to one topology means excluding it from the other view, which a scenario concept would remove
and which is recorded rather than hidden.

## A judgement whose basis was never read

`record_factor_assessment` already refused an *empty* basis digest, for an exact reason: without a
basis there is nothing to retire the judgement, so it would apply forever. There is a second way to
have no basis and it fails the other way round — the judgement applies never.

`read_architecture_basis` yields the empty `ArchitectureBasis()` when the architecture model or the
connection types cannot be reached, which the assurance surfaces need in order to run standalone. Its
occurrence digest was `compute_basis_digest([])`: a well-formed hash, and one that no reader with the
model in hand would ever compute. A judgement recorded against it was born superseded.

Eleven judgements in the shipped store were in exactly that state, each with a written rationale, and
none had ever applied. Nothing said so, because the finding for a retired judgement reads *made
against a picture that has since changed* — which sends a reader looking for a change that never
happened.

The marker cannot be the hash. An element with no connections in a basis that *was* assembled
legitimately cites no facts and hashes to the same value, so refusing the hash would refuse a correct
judgement about an isolated element and admit an ungrounded one. `ArchitectureBasis` now says whether
it was assembled; an unassembled one yields `UNGROUNDED_BASIS`, which is a word rather than 32 hex
characters and can collide with nothing. `derive_factors` takes `None` for "not knowable" as against
`()` for "read, and cites nothing" — two types rather than a flag, because it is a property of the
input. Severity and detectability are untouched either way: both derive from the assurance graph, so a
severity correction is still recordable on a standalone bridge.

The eleven were re-recorded against the assembled model, with their values and rationales unchanged
and a note on each saying it was re-grounded mechanically rather than re-reviewed.

## Which half of a finding containment answers

W511 says an element *appears in no control structure and has no failure modes*. One sentence, two
absences, and they do not want the same rule — which is why suppressing on a single flat set was
wrong in a way that took a measurement to see.

The set was the elements some assurance node binds to **directly**. So a component composed by an
analysed controller reported as an analysis gap: 7 of 24 findings named elements composed by
`APP@1777293133.OYEmP1`, whose control-structure node has its process model, control algorithm and
feedback written out in full. Each finding's own witness printed the composition edge that contradicted
it.

**Containment answers the first absence.** An STPA control structure is coarser than the component
decomposition on purpose — Leveson models it where control decisions are made — and what was analysed
is the controller's algorithm and process model, which cover its parts. Drawing all 24 in would not
produce a control structure; it would reproduce the component diagram.

**It must not answer the second.** A failure mode is per-component by definition, and the parent having
them says nothing about the child. Expanding there would silence exactly what FMEA is for. So **W510 is
not given the expanded set**: it reports an element the control structure *names*, and a part of a named
controller is not named. Without that separation the change would have moved seven info findings into
seven warnings — worse than the finding it set out to fix.

`elements_within` walks declared containment downward only. What a container holds is part of it; the
container an element belongs to is not part of the element, so walking up would say that analysing a
component analysed the system around it. Which relations mean containment is injected rather than
spelled: composition and aggregation carry `relationship_kind: containment`, and the derivation picked
up the datatype notation's own pair as well without this layer naming either module.

There is a second downward-containment walk in the codebase, in the C4 roll-up, and it is not reused
here on purpose: it traverses a `ModelQuery`, and the assurance basis holds typed edges. Two inputs,
two walks, one decision — recorded so the next reader does not take it for an oversight.

Measured after: W511 24 → 17, exactly the seven predicted, W510 unchanged.

## A recorded defect that was not one

*A C4 group draws flat where its members sit a level deeper* was on the backlog with a proposed fix:
roll a grouping up so that one whose members all lie inside a single drawn container becomes a nested
boundary within it. Measured before building it, and neither half of the proposal survives.

All five groupings under the Architecture Backend have **every** member rolling up to that one
container. Nothing spans containers, so the "boundary around several containers" branch has no
instance; and the other branch would draw five empty labelled boxes inside one box, which is what
`groupings_without_members` exists to prevent.

The mechanism the entry blamed is not even reached. A grouping only becomes a candidate if it is a
*direct child of the view's root*, and these are children of the backend — so on a container view of
the platform they are gone before membership is consulted.

What the behaviour actually is: a group is drawn on the view whose root is its parent, holding the
members that level draws. That is C4's own rule — a group holds elements of one abstraction level — and
a container view showing a container's internal groupings would mix two. All five are drawn on the
backend's component view with their members inside.

So the release pins the rule instead of changing it, and the test's last assertion is the interesting
one: it fails the day a grouping *does* span containers, which reopens the question with the live case
that was missing.

## Findings computed, counted, and withheld

`redact_verification` kept a verification issue when its `node_id` was among the *visible assurance
node ids*. Every two-way coverage finding names the **architecture element** it is about — that is its
subject, and it is what makes the finding readable — and an architecture element id is never an
assurance node id.

So all of them were dropped: 17 of 20 issues on the shipped store, every one a W511, leaving only the
three whose subject happens to be an assurance node. They were derived, counted, and then removed
before the response was built, from a reader entitled to every one. The half of the verifier that
compares the two models has therefore been invisible in the product — which is also why the
containment fix above could not be seen there.

The rule the filter meant to state was already in its own docstring: *an issue naming no node is about
the store itself and stays, there is no subject to withhold.* An issue naming something that is not an
assurance node is the same case, and it was not treated as one. Telling the two apart needs the full
node set — "not an assurance node" against "an assurance node held back" — so the caller supplies it,
and its absence keeps the old closed behaviour, because a confidentiality boundary must not open by
omission.

**Loosening a confidentiality filter, argued rather than asserted.** A coverage finding discloses two
things: architecture facts, which carry no TLP, and the *absence* of assurance content. Nor can it
disclose withheld content by inference — the finding is computed over the whole store, so it fires only
where nothing is bound at all. An element analysed by a node above the ceiling produces no finding, and
a finding that does not exist says nothing about what does.

Found by asking where in the GUI the containment fix would be visible, and getting no answer.

## A lane that carries its identity

A swimlane may be bound and the binding persisted, while the renderer wrote a bare `|Label|`. So an
action, a decision and a partition were all selectable and the lane alone was not, in the same module
— which is what made it read as an oversight rather than a decision.

Whether the notation permits it had to be settled first, because the module records the opposite
precedent for forks: `fork [[url]]` is a syntax error, which is why a fork and a join are drawn as
bare bars with nothing on them to read. A lane could have been the same case. Measured on the pinned
PlantUML 1.2026.3, on a two-lane body whose first header reads `|[[arch://ln_ops Operations]]|`: one
anchor with that href, and both headers keeping their own text. A lane is not a fork.

Two places wrote that line — the renderer for the first lane, the emission walk for every switch after
it — so wiring one would have left the first lane as the only unclickable one, which is worse than the
bug. `lane_header` is the one emission, beside the sentinel it writes.

**`drawn_step_ids` stops counting lanes, and that is the load-bearing part.** A bound lane now carries
a sentinel, and W045 asks whether every declared *step* is drawn; a lane counted as a step makes that
question unanswerable. `drawn_lane_ids` answers the other half, so one syntax still has one reader and
the round trip is stated over both halves rather than over the one that existed first.

`puml_text` moved out of `_emission` because `_step_links` needs it and `_emission` imports
`_step_links` — leaving it would have made the pair circular. That settled a question the backlog had
left open about its home: the shared part is what any PUML line needs, which is
`puml_line_text`, and the sequence renderer's byte-identical copy is gone. The `|` → `/` rule stayed
with the activity module, because only a swimlane header reserves that character.

## Fork spacing: a fix that would have done nothing

An activity diagram's fork edges turn against the bar and overlap it, and the recorded fix was to give
`activity/config.yaml` a `layout.node_sep` and `layout.rank_sep` and emit them beside the wrap
skinparams, the way `wrap_width` already works.

Measured before building it, on a three-branch fork across two lanes:

| body | result |
| --- | --- |
| baseline | 671x339 |
| `skinparam ranksep 400` | 671x339 — unchanged |
| `nodesep 70` + `ranksep 120` | unchanged |
| `spacing`, `PageMargin`, `ColorArrowSeparationSpace` | unchanged |
| `<style> activityDiagram { Margin 20 }` | unchanged |
| `skinparam padding 20` | 851x505 — pads box *interiors*, and PlantUML draws "Please use CSS style instead of skinparam padding" into the image |

So the two keys would emit two inert skinparams, and a dead knob is worse than an absent one: the next
reader sets it, sees nothing, and has to repeat this measurement to learn why.

The parallel the report drew is false, and that is the finding. The ArchiMate renderer's `nodesep 60 /
ranksep 80` does work — the same pair takes a three-rectangle diagram from 89x248 to 108x368. ArchiMate
views are laid out by GraphViz; activity-beta has its own engine and honours neither parameter.

What the measurement did establish is narrower and more useful: **the overlap is specific to a fork
whose branches cross swimlanes.** A same-lane fork draws three straight vertical arrows with no turns
at all. Cross-lane, the routing puts its horizontal segment at the bar's own y, so it abuts the bar at
both the fork and the join — and declaring every branch's lane explicitly changes nothing.

No warning is raised for it. A fork whose branches span roles is correct modelling, and reporting it
would blame an author for a renderer limitation.

## The includes an upgrade never refreshed

`_archimate-stereotypes.puml` and its two siblings are generated files committed into a repository.
Every diagram `!include`s them and their content is the ontology's declarations rendered into
skinparams and macros, so a release that changes a declaration reaches an existing repository only when
they are rewritten.

Nothing rewrote them on upgrade. `arch-init` regenerates them and CI checks them; `arch-repair
upgrade`, whose whole job is bringing a repository to the current version, did not. This release's
grouping notation would therefore have stopped at the repository boundary — the ontology saying dashed
and unfilled while the file every diagram includes still said filled with a solid border — and so would
every appearance change before it, back to the release that made the colours declarations.

Not an upgrade step, and the layering is the reason: a step's replacement content comes from the
domain, and this content is generated from the module registry, which an application-layer step may not
reach. The CLI is the composition root that already does this in `arch-init`. It runs after the steps
apply, because a step can change what the generator would emit and regenerating first would bake the
pre-migration answer in.

## Two deciders, four times over

Every search symptom in this release turned out to be a second mechanism overriding a first that had
already answered correctly, and reading either one alone made the product look right.

`prioritize_global_hits` put every non-entity record in a bucket behind all entities, which silently
undid `_rank_balanced` — the use case that ranks within each kind and round-robins across them
*because* bm25 scores and the token-match supplement are on incomparable scales. With a window of
twenty and forty entity hits, a diagram could not appear at all, though the index ranked it top of its
kind. `/api/search` then asked the use case for three times the caller's limit and re-cut the result
after re-ordering it, so both of the use case's guarantees — the round-robin and the floor that keeps a
minority kind present — were spent on rows nobody saw. Each of the three was defensible on its own
terms; together they were a ranking nobody had decided.

Scratchpads were the same shape twice. `merge_search_rows` interleaves the engagement and enterprise
rows by iterating a tuple of record types, and the tuple named four where the vocabulary has five: every
`scratchpad-note` row was dropped whenever both roots were merged, which is every search the backend
serves. That did not make notes unfindable, which is why it survived — `search` runs a scored supplement
for any kind with no FTS hits, notes always had none, so notes arrived by the supplement every time. And
the supplement matched a note's own title and body and never its scratchpad's name. So a note was
findable by its own words, a pad was not findable by its title, and the FTS weights for notes were never
consulted in the product at all.

The remedy in each case was to delete the second decider rather than reconcile it: a stable sort on one
key, the caller's own limit, and a record-type order derived from the vocabulary instead of restated
beside it.

### One transposed weight list

`scratchpad_notes_fts` has seven columns and its bm25 call passed six weights. fts5 accepts a short
list, treats the missing trailing weights as nothing, and ranks. The list omitted `scratchpad_id`, so
everything after it landed one column early: the UNINDEXED id absorbed the title's 4.0, the title took
the body's 0.5, and `scratchpad_name` got nothing.

Measured on a two-row table, a note matched on its scratchpad's *name* outranked one matched on its own
**title**, both at ~1e-7. Aligning the list reversed the order and raised the scores tenfold. Nothing
in the schema or the query would have said so, which is why the fix ships with a gate that reads each
weight list against the DDL that creates its table: 5 cases, one per weighted table plus the two that
are deliberately unweighted.

## A flag that defaults to safe and is read once

`artifact_group` declared `dry_run` with a default of `True`. One branch of one action read it — the
cascade delete of a model project. Create, rename, archive, unarchive, update, and the deletion of a
diagram- or document-collection all mutated the repository whatever it said, and the result named
neither the flag nor the outcome: `{"action": "created", …}` is exactly what a faithful preview would
also answer.

Every operation in that module has one shape — load the registry, validate, compute the axis' new entry
list, commit — so a preview is that code with the effects withheld. The decision moved to a single seam
carrying the three effects an operation can have, and the six handlers lost the branch entirely.
`group_delete_collection` derives its own policy rather than accepting one, because it is the action
that already took `dry_run`, and two parameters for one decision can disagree: a caller passing only
`dry_run=True` would have got the deletion it asked to preview.

The two new result keys are required fields on a closed response model, so the REST group routes answer
500 without them — four route tests show it. The contract gate did not, because it reads `return {…}`
literals carrying an `action` key and the dispatch answers `{**result, …}`. That is the third shape to
hide behind that extraction, after the cascade envelope; it now reads the stamped keys too.

## Exclusivity was in the ontology and in no rule

ArchiMate 4 distinguishes composition from aggregation by exclusivity, and this ontology says so in the
relation's own guidance: a composition holds when the target is an integral part of the source "and of
that whole alone … no second whole may claim it", and the shared case is aggregation. Nothing checked
it. A model asserting that one part constitutes two wholes passed every pass, incremental and full, at
zero errors and zero warnings.

`relationship_kind` could not carry the check: composition and aggregation are both `containment`, and
exclusivity is the whole of the difference between them. So the connection type gains
`exclusive_target`, declared by `archimate-composition` and by the datatype notation's
`dt-composition`, read by both ontology loaders. The rule names no relation, which is what lets it
apply to whatever declares the property.

It is repository-level rather than per-file because the two wholes are two *files*: each declares the
part in its own `outgoing.md` and neither is wrong alone — the shape a per-file verifier structurally
cannot see. Two rules in this release have that property, and both had to avoid the same trap: on the
`verify_all` path the candidate repository is point-lookup only and its `list_*` answer `[]` by design,
so a rule enumerating content through it is silent in the product and green against a test that hands
it a real store.

## A commit that could carry part of a batch

The staged transaction derives its manifest from the paths the writes reported touching, and only
`model/`, `docs/`, `diagram-catalog/` and `projects/` can appear in it. A touched path outside those was
dropped there without a word — by which point the write had happened in the staged root, had answered
`wrote: true`, and the batch verification had passed.

No operation reaches it: bulk write and bulk delete are the only users of the staged transaction, and
instrumenting a run of each produced no unrepresentable touched path. It is closed anyway, as an error
naming the paths and raised before anything is published, because a partial commit that reports success
is the one outcome a transaction must not produce.
