## An activity loop is drawn or refused, and the criterion describes what the emitter draws

The emitter hands a loop's body to the ordinary emission with the condition as a stop, so a decision,
a fork, a partition or a note inside a loop is drawn by the same code as one outside it. The
acceptance criterion described something narrower: a chain of single-successor steps, which ends at
the first branch. The accounting invariant then refused the remainder as unaccounted for. A retry
loop whose body branches was refused for a limit the notation does not impose.

The criterion now describes the region the emission walks. Two further rules replace a reading that
treated leaving the strongly connected component as leaving the loop:

- the loop's foot is the decision with exactly one arm inside the cycle. Under the old reading a
  decision in the *body* counted as a second exit, because its arms end in steps that never return;
- one step may return to the header, which refuses two back edges. That shape had rendered as a
  repeat with an empty arm and one return relocated into the body.

Two shapes the wider criterion admits are drawn wrongly by PlantUML and are refused with the reason:

- two nested loops form **one** strongly connected component, so every step is accounted for inside
  the outer shape while the inner way back is drawn nowhere;
- a body arm reaching a step that comes *after* the loop pulls that step and its whole continuation
  inside the repeat, so the picture runs them on every pass and the loop's exit draws nothing.

Both are rows in the shape matrix, alongside the return path of more than one step and the two flows
returning to one step.

A loop is drawn only when every step of its cycle sits in one swimlane. A repeat spanning two lanes
has its way back routed through the boxes of its own body, ending inside the header instead of at its
edge. The same shape in one lane draws cleanly.

## Reading the drawing, not only the body

Every other check on a diagram reads the declarations or the PUML. Both are blind to what the layout
engine does with them: a body can be valid, lay out without a warning, and still draw an arrow that
stops in mid-air, an arm that merges into another arm, or a return edge routed through the box it
returns to.

`src/infrastructure/rendering/drawn_connections.py` reads a rendered SVG's geometry and reports three
kinds of disconnection: an arrow reaching nothing, an arrow landing partway along a line running
crosswise to it, and a line crossing a shape's interior. A mid-edge direction marker is not one — its
tip sits on a line collinear with the direction it points, which is how the two are separated.

The module is generic and carries no diagram-type vocabulary. Its blind spot is stated in the module:
a rendered SVG carries no edge identity, so an arrow merging into a line parallel to itself cannot be
distinguished from a marker, and is not reported. The gate is stated over the whole catalogue of
shapes the notation permits, and carries two bodies the product refuses to emit — each renders without
a warning and draws a broken flow — so a change that blinds the check fails it.

The two loop shapes above score zero on this check. Their pictures connect; what is wrong is what the
flow means. That bounds what geometry can be asked to answer.

## A diagram keeps what a write was not asked to change

`format_diagram_puml` writes only what it is given, so a field omitted is a field deleted. The project
cascade delete passed eleven arguments of twenty. Every foreign diagram it touched lost its
`keywords`, `authored-groupings`, `bindings`, `edge-labels`, `viewpoint`, `view_derivations`,
`diagram-format-version`, `manual-layout` — which is what keeps a hand-laid body from being
regenerated — and `tlp`, a confidentiality classification. The operation reported success and the
file verified afterwards.

The mapping between a diagram's frontmatter keys and those arguments is named once, in the module that
owns the frontmatter shape. Held at each call site, it goes out of date silently.

`tests/architecture/test_a_diagram_rewrite_carries_its_frontmatter.py` records which call sites
rewrite a stored file and which create a new one. A rewrite must spread the carry-over. A create may
omit a carried field, and must not accept one from its caller and then drop it — the case where "it is
a create" would otherwise excuse a real loss. A third rule keeps the register current as the formatter
grows: every field the formatter can write is either carried across a rewrite or restated by one.

## Nested groupings, and a grouping member as a reference

Grouping boxes nest to any depth, which the served contract states. The sync rebuilt each box from
three keys, and `groups` was not among them, so a nested box was discarded: `Outer[a] > Inner[b, c]`
came back as `Outer[a]`, with no warning. A box now survives if it keeps a member or keeps a box, and
keys it does not know about pass through.

An entity a diagram places in a grouping is referenced by that diagram as an `entity-ids-used` entry
is. The dependency guard read only the top list, so deleting an entity referenced solely through a
grouping was neither blocked nor reported, and the id survived pointing at nothing. The guard reads
every level, and the delete prunes what it removed — required once the rewrite carries groupings
across.

Two traversals of that tree existed under two names, one for each job. There is one now, in the
domain. It takes the predicate and returns what it removed as data, leaving the vocabulary and the
warning wording with the callers that own them.

## Correcting a diagram's recorded references

`connection-ids-used` answers *which views show this connection*, so a wrong entry is a wrong answer
about the model. On a manual-layout ArchiMate diagram it could be neither corrected nor reported.

**The write half.** The reconcile drops a stored reference only where the body positively contradicts
it. That is correct for an inference drawn from a body, and it was applied to the caller's own
argument as well, so a supplied list was merged into the surviving stored set instead of replacing it.
A hand-laid ArchiMate body draws plain arrows with no stereotype, so nothing is ever contradicted and
every stale reference survived. A caller who passes the set is stating it, so the stored value is no
longer carried. `entity_ids` was already honoured in that position.

**The reporting half.** W307 had one ground for a contradiction: the body declares both endpoints and
draws no relation between them. That ground is silent when an endpoint is missing, so every reference
to an entity a re-cut view had dropped escaped the rule. A second ground reports an endpoint appearing
in neither the body nor `entity-ids-used`, since nothing in that body can reach it. The evidence is
what makes it sound: an entity a body draws must appear in `entity-ids-used`, so an endpoint missing
from both is absent in fact and not merely unresolved. Where a caller offers no entity list the ground
does not apply, an absent list being no evidence at all.

## What is documented, and where

Both rules above are on every surface an author reaches: the ArchiMate type's own `puml_notes`, served
by `artifact_authoring_guidance`; the `artifact_edit_diagram` description; the diagramming
documentation; and W307's message, which names the correction. The documentation also records what
`auto-sync` does not do on a manual-layout diagram: it corrects reference spellings and reconciles
bindings, and leaves the reference lists alone.

Activity guidance says what a drawn loop may hold — how a loop is closed, that the return path carries
one step, that two returns to one step cannot be drawn, and that the cycle must stay in one lane.

## Known limitations, restated accurately

PlantUML's activity family lays out internally and does not consult GraphViz. `nodesep` and `ranksep`
have no effect there: geometry is identical at every value from 5 to 300, where the same parameters
resize a class diagram.

The previous wording — that the activity layout honours no spacing parameter — is falsifiable. A
deprecated skinparam and a CSS style block do change activity spacing, and neither helps: the
deprecated one draws its own warning into the image, and the gap between a fork bar and its branch
edges' turn is a fixed 4 px at every value, so a larger canvas makes it proportionally smaller.
