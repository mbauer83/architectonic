## Context

The requirement [Semantic Full-Text Search](../../projects/motivation-narrative/model/motivation/requirement/REQ@1712870400.Cc2Dd2.semantic-full-text-search.md) has been satisfied so far by keyword ranking alone. A vector retriever fused with that keyword search closes the gap it names, and two questions had to be answered before any of it could be built: which model produces the vectors, and whose content is allowed to reach it.

Both were answered by measurement rather than preference.

**What the corpus is.** 669 records — entities, documents, diagrams — give 1,158 chunks of about 512 characters. The embedding matrix is under 2 MB at any dimensionality worth using. That is small enough that several designs which would otherwise be necessary are not.

**What keyword search already answers.** Against labels derived from the model itself, keyword recall@10 is 99.2% when the query is an artifact's own name and 97.5% when it is its own summary. There is no headroom in either. Where headroom exists is the query that shares no vocabulary with its answer: asked to find the component realising a requirement from the requirement's wording alone, keyword recall@10 is 3.3%.

**What a transformer costs.** An ONNX transformer encoder needs `onnxruntime`, whose dependency closure is 248 MB across 13 distributions — `sympy` alone is 74 MB and has nothing to do with embedding. It encodes a query in 10.5 ms and builds the corpus in 15.5 s.

**What a static encoder costs.** A distilled static model — a token-to-vector lookup with pooling, no transformer forward pass — needs numpy, a tokenizer and safetensors. It encodes a query in 0.04 ms and builds the corpus in 0.2 s.

**How they compare on relevance.** On the queries with headroom, fused recall@10 rises from 3.3% to 12.5% with the static encoder and to 10.0% with the transformer. On the queries without headroom neither regresses. The transformer is better by under a percentage point on the vector list alone and worse fused; at 120 queries per stratum those differences are two or three queries and this suite cannot distinguish them from noise.

## Decision

**Vectors come from a static embedding model, held and applied entirely within the deployment.** No query, artifact, or fragment of either is sent to a hosted embedding service. The model is a local asset; a search performs no network access.

**The weights are an installed asset, not a dependency.** A lock file records Python distributions; it does not record a model file, so the weights need their own answer. They are acquired as a content-addressed asset with its digest and licence recorded beside the manifest, installed by the deployment and upgrade path, and verified on load. A missing file, a corrupt file and a digest mismatch each disable vector retrieval and say so; none of them degrades keyword search, which remains the whole of what the product promises without them.

**The confidential assurance store is excluded from the vector index entirely.** Not embedded locally with an inherited classification — excluded. The [Confidential Assurance Store](../../projects/assurance/model/application/application-component/APP@1780656431.E0fzqZ.confidential-assurance-store.md) exists because that content is separated from the engagement repository, and an embedding is a derived representation of the text that produced it: one index holding both tiers would put a derivative of classified content in a file governed by the engagement repository's rules, where a search answering across the corpus could surface its existence through a similarity score. Vector retrieval covers the engagement and enterprise repositories. Assurance content is searched by the assurance surface, under its own classification ceiling.

**There is no persisted embedding cache.** A 0.2-second build over the whole corpus is rebuilt in memory when the process starts. That decision removes a file, a content-addressed generation scheme, an atomically replaced pointer, a grace period before collecting a superseded generation, and the staleness question all of them exist to answer.

**The encoder is replaceable behind one contract.** Retrieval is expressed as a ranked list of typed candidates; nothing above it knows what produced the ranks. Changing encoder changes one class.

## Consequences

**Keyword search remains the guarantee, and vector retrieval an improvement on it.** The two are combined by reciprocal rank fusion rather than by scaling one retriever's scores into the other's range, because their scales do not compare — a fact this codebase had already reached by ordering search results across kinds by round-robin. Fusion below the existing ranking policy leaves that policy's guarantees intact.

**Relevance is now falsifiable, and that is a standing obligation.** The labelled suite that produced the figures above is the instrument for any later claim about search quality, including a change of model. Its non-regression floor is the two strata where keyword search already succeeds. Without it, "better" is an opinion.

**One cost of fusion is accepted with open eyes.** On queries where the keyword retriever contributes almost nothing, fusing its list with the vector list scores worse than the vector list alone. Equal-weight fusion dilutes a good list with a bad one. It is accepted because the strata where keyword search is excellent are the common case and fusion protects them; if that trade is revisited, the suite is what decides it.

**What the figures above measure, and what a reader actually gets.** The recall figures in Context were taken over candidate lists, before the ordering policy. Measured through the shipped read path at recall@10, on the same stratum: asking for components, keyword alone answers 10.8%, the vector list alone 14.2%, and the two fused 13.3% — the accepted dilution, reproduced. Asking across every kind, keyword answers 2.5% and fusion 3.3%, because the cross-kind round-robin gives an entity fewer than three of the ten rows and this stratum's answer is always an entity. So "3.3% to 12.5%" compares a windowed result against a candidate list and describes neither on its own. The lift is real and it is about two and a half points where the question is about components.

**The instrument is committed, and it is what settles any later claim.** `tools/quality/relevance_suite.py` derives the strata from whatever the repository currently holds rather than from a stored list of pairs, which would go stale at the first rename. The floor it feeds asserts that fusion loses nothing where keyword search already answers, and that it gains something where keyword search does not — relations between two configurations over one corpus, never a rate against a remembered number.

**The requirement's wording is now wrong about its own implementation.** It says the mechanism "may use TF-IDF ranking or vector-based similarity, backed by the SQLite runtime index". Vector similarity is not backed by that index; it is a separate matrix built from a separate model. A requirement should not name the mechanism that realises it, and this one names two. Correcting it is model work this decision creates.

**A static encoder has a lower ceiling than a transformer, and the corpus may outgrow the choice.** The measurements above are of this corpus at this size. The suite and the replaceable contract are what make revisiting cheap; the 248 MB closure is what makes revisiting a decision rather than a default.
